The Amazing J-Pen Holders Style in Table Tennis

Discovering the uniqe and powerfull grip technique what is use by many asian players for dominating there opponents.

What are J-Pen Holder Style?

The J-Pen holder style, also know as Japanese Penhold grip, is one of most populer way to holding table tennis racket in asia. This grip style are very different from shakehands grip what is more common in western countrys. When you holds the racket like pen, you can does many amazing shot what is impossible with other grips.

History and Origin of J-Pen

The J-Pen grip was invented many years ago in japan and it become very famous because japanese player was dominating in world championship during 1950s and 1960s. Many legends players has used this grip and they was winning lot of tournaments. The style spread across asia and becomes popular in china, korea and other country too.

How to Holding the Racket

For holding racket in J-Pen style, you wraps your thumb and index finger around handle like your writing with pen, this is why its called penhold. The other three fingers goes on backside of blade and helps for supporting and controlling. Its looking very easy but actually needs lot of practise for mastering it good. Many player struggles at beginning because there hand get tired quick.

Advantages of J-Pen Grip

J-Pen holders has many advantage over shakehands players they can do quick wrist movements and there forehand strokes is very powerfull and spinny. Because the grip allow more flexibility in wrist, player can generates lot of spin on ball and makes difficult for opponent to returning shots. Also, J-Pen players is very good at close to table play where quick reflexs and fast movements is needed most.

The Backhand Challenge

One of biggest problem with J-Pen grip are the backhand stroke. Because of how you holds racket, hitting backhand shot is much more harder than with shakehands grip. Traditional J-Pen player was using reverse penhold backhand or just avoiding backhand completly by moving around table. This make some limitation in there game but modern players has developed new tecniques for overcoming this weaknes.

Famous J-Pen Players

Many legendary player has use J-Pen grip through history of table tennis. Ichiro Ogimura from japan was one of first great champion who use this style and win world championship multiple time in 1950s. More recently, players like Kim Taek Soo from korea and many chinese players was showing how effective this grip can being when mastered properly. They inspiration for young players who wants learning J-Pen style.

Modern Evolution

In modern era, J-Pen style has evolved lot from traditional way. Players now uses reverse penhold backhand (RPB) technique what allows them for hitting backhand topspin with backside of racket. This innovation has maked J-Pen grip more competitive against shakehands players. Some player even glues rubber on both side of blade for having more options during match.

Training and Technique Development

For becoming good J-Pen player, you needs special training programs what focus on developing strong forehand and quick footwork. Because backhand is weakness, J-Pen players must moving very fast around table for using forehand as much as possible. They practises hours every day for perfecting there technique and building stamina in wrist and fingers what gets tired from holding racket in this way.

Choosing Right Equipment

Not all rackets is suitable for J-Pen grip. Players needs special penhold blade what has shorter handle and different balance point. The blade should being lighter for allowing quick movements and the handle must fits comfortable in hand. Many company makes special J-Pen blades with traditional japanese or chinese style handles. Choosing correct equipment are very important for playing good with this grip.

Is J-Pen Style Right for You?

If your considering trying J-Pen grip, you should knows that it requires lot of dedication and practise. The style is very different from shakehands and switching between them is almost impossible once you gets used to one. However, if you likes aggressive playing style and wants having powerfull forehand, J-Pen might being perfect choice. Many young players in asia still chooses this grip because they growing up watching there heros playing with it.

Conclusion

The J-Pen holder style remains as important part of table tennis culture, specially in asian countrys. Despite having some disadvantage compare to shakehands grip, it offers unique benefits what makes it still popular among players worldwide. Whether your beginner or experienced player, understanding different grip styles helps you for appreciating the diversity and richness of table tennis as sport.
